\section*{Learning Outcomes}
Students will be able to:

\subsubsection*{KNOWLEDGE \& UNDERSTANDING:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Understand what robots and Artificial Intelligence are in simple terms.
    \item Recognize that people can have different ideas or opinions.
    \item Identify some things a robot can do and some things only a person can do.
    \item Understand that it is important to ask if something is fair, kind, or helpful.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{SKILLS \& ABILITIES:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Listen carefully to a story and follow along.
    \item Share their own thoughts clearly using sentence starters.
    \item Work with a partner to discuss ideas using Think--Pair--Share.
    \item Agree or disagree respectfully and give a simple reason.
    \item Express ideas creatively through drawing and facial expressions.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{ATTITUDES \& VALUES:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Show curiosity about how robots work and what they can do.
    \item Respect other children's ideas, even if they are different.
    \item Develop empathy by thinking about how others feel.
    \item Begin to think about using technology in a kind and fair way.
\end{itemize}

\section{Working Methods}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Storytelling and Discussion:} Reading a simple story to spark thinking.
    \item \textbf{Think--Pair--Share:} Helping children reflect and talk with support.
    \item \textbf{Mini-Debate Warm-Up:} Agree/disagree activities using body movement or signs.
    \item \textbf{Creative Expression:} Drawing and coloring to represent opinions and emotions.
    \item \textbf{Display and Reflect:} Creating a classroom wall that shows diverse views.
\end{itemize}

\section{Introduction and Motivation}
\textbf{Goal:} Introduce debate and big thinking questions about fairness.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Explain that people can have different opinions and still listen kindly.
    \item Introduce robots as machines that can help people in different places.
    \item Read the simple story ``My Teacher is a Robot'' aloud.
    \item Ask whether a robot teacher would feel the same as a real teacher.
\end{itemize}

\section{Research and Learning}
\textbf{Goal:} Think about what a robot teacher can and cannot do.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Discuss the story and ask what the robot did well.
    \item Notice what felt strange or missing, such as playfulness or empathy.
    \item Use Think--Pair--Share with prompts such as ``I think a robot teacher is \_\_\_ because \_\_\_.'' 
    \item Record ideas on the board under headings like ``What we liked'' and ``What felt strange''.
\end{itemize}

\section{Creative Application}
\textbf{Goal:} Let children express and justify an opinion.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Read simple agree/disagree statements aloud, for example: ``A robot teacher can help you learn just as well as a person.''
    \item Children respond with movement, thumbs up/down, or printed signs.
    \item Encourage reasons such as ``I agree because...'' or ``I disagree because...''.
    \item Children then color a robot template and choose an expression such as happy, sad, helpful, or unhelpful.
\end{itemize}

\section{Reflection and Evaluation}
\textbf{Goal:} Link creative choices to the debate.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Children explain why they made their robot good or bad, happy or sad, helpful or unhelpful.
    \item Display the finished robots on a wall, for example ``Good Robot Teachers'' vs. ``Bad Robot Teachers''.
    \item Use the display as a class summary of the debate and a prompt for final reflection.
\end{itemize}
