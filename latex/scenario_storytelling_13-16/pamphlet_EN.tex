\pamphletheading{Introduction}
Whose story is it?

In this workshop, students (13-16) dive into the Age of Discovery. They use AI to research explorers, analyze map bias, and create digital stories (podcasts \& maps) that challenge traditional narratives and explore the roots of globalization.

\vfill\null
\pamphletheading{Key Goals}
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=SmaileLightTeal, colframe=SmaileTeal, boxrule=0.5pt, arc=2mm]
\small
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Analyze:} Colonialism \& Trade.
    \item \textbf{Critique:} Map bias (Mercator).
    \item \textbf{Create:} Indigenous Diaries.
    \item \textbf{Map:} Interactive StoryMaps.
\end{itemize}
\end{tcolorbox}

% --- Panel 5: Activities ---
\pamphletheading{1. Critical Research}
\textbf{Explorer Profiles:}
Using AI to investigate motivations and consequences of explorers like Columbus.
\textbf{Map Bias:}
Comparing maps to see how "centers" of power are created visually.

\pamphletheading{2. Creative Storytelling}
\textbf{Indigenous Diary:}
Writing a script from the perspective of an indigenous observer.
\textbf{StoryMapJS:}
Creating an interactive digital map that traces the journey of a commodity (e.g., Chocolate) across the globe.
